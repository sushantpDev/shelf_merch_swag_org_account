# Company Swag Store: How Enterprise Budgets and Employee Points Work

A company swag store is an online storefront where employees can choose branded merchandise selected for their organization. In a points-based program, employees receive points and redeem them for available products. For an enterprise, the store also needs a clear operating model: who controls the company budget, who manages each entity, and who processes employee orders.

ShelfMerch's enterprise workflow connects those steps. A company begins with a demo, receives a company admin and an assigned budget, and creates entity managers with allocated budgets. Managers can create stores using catalog products or send kits. They can also distribute employee points from their budgets. Employees visit the store, redeem their points for merchandise, and place orders for ShelfMerch to process.

This guide explains that workflow and the decisions to make before launching a program.

## Start with the program, then build the store

Before selecting merchandise, agree on the purpose of the program. Is it an ongoing employee merch store, a new hire initiative, or a defined gifting campaign? That decision shapes the audience, ownership, and budget conversation.

An ongoing store may need a different planning process from a one-time welcome kit. The first gives employees a place to choose from available merchandise. The second begins with a curated selection. Both need someone accountable for the program.

Write a short brief that answers three questions:

- Who is the program for?
- Who owns its budget and day-to-day decisions?
- What should employees be able to do when they receive their invitation?

Use that brief during the demo. It gives the discussion a concrete starting point and helps your team ask about the workflow it actually needs.

## Understand the company admin and entity manager roles

In the ShelfMerch setup described here, the company admin is created as part of enterprise onboarding and the company receives an assigned budget. The company can then create multiple entity managers and allocate budgets to them.

An entity manager is the person responsible for a defined part of the program. Map that responsibility to your own organizational structure during setup. For example, your planning may distinguish business units, departments, or other groups; confirm how those groups should be represented before configuring them.

### Define ownership before allocating budgets

Name the people responsible for the company-level program and each participating entity. Decide who will answer employee questions, coordinate product selection, and discuss order issues with ShelfMerch.

These are operating decisions for your team. Do not assume that naming someone a program owner automatically creates an approval rule or an integration. Ask to see the actual permissions and available controls during the demo.

### Keep the company budget and entity allocations distinct

The company budget is the overall starting point. An entity allocation is the portion assigned to a manager's area. Employee points are a further part of the distribution and redemption journey.

Treat these as separate concepts when discussing the program. A budget allocation does not mean an employee has already selected a product or placed an order. Clear terminology makes it easier to ask useful questions about what happens at each stage.

## Add catalog products to the company store

Managers can create a store and add products from the catalog. Product selection should support the purpose of the store and the needs of the intended employees.

Begin with a focused selection rather than adding products simply to make the store look larger. Check the current options with ShelfMerch and consider whether employees can make a meaningful choice within the points they receive.

Before announcing the store, review the actual product information. Confirm the available options, branding requirements, and any applicable commercial or fulfillment details. Catalog availability and service terms should come from the current offering, rather than a general assumption about what a merch platform usually provides.

## Distribute points from allocated budgets

In a points-based program, managers distribute points to employees from their allocated budgets. Employees can use those points to redeem merchandise through the store.

### Decide how employees will understand the program

Prepare a short explanation of what the points are for, where employees should go, and whom they can contact for help. Keep this explanation consistent with the actual program settings.

If your organization wants rules about allocation amounts, eligibility, or campaign timing, discuss those requirements before launch. Point value, expiry, payment combinations, and exceptions need explicit confirmation. They should not be inferred from the word "points."

### Separate distribution from redemption

Giving an employee points and receiving an employee order are different events. An employee may receive points before deciding what to choose. Your internal program review should distinguish points distributed, employee redemptions, and orders being processed.

That distinction is useful even when your organization uses its own reporting process. It keeps conversations about participation separate from conversations about order handling.

## Let employees choose and redeem merchandise

Once points have been distributed, employees visit the store and choose from its available merchandise. They redeem points and place their orders through the store workflow.

The employee communication should make the next step clear. Explain the store's purpose, how to access it, and where to find support. Avoid adding policies to the invitation that have not been agreed with the program owner and ShelfMerch.

Before sending the invitation broadly, walk through the actual employee journey with the implementation team. Check that the store content and program instructions match. If a step is unclear to your internal team, resolve it before employees encounter it.

## Know what happens after an order is placed

ShelfMerch receives and processes employee orders. For your program team, the practical next step is to agree how order questions will be handled and what information employees should expect to receive.

Ask about the applicable fulfillment process, service area, timelines, and support arrangements for your specific program. These details depend on the actual offering and order requirements; a points-based store does not by itself establish a delivery promise.

Include the order handoff in your launch planning. A complete program explains both how employees choose merchandise and what happens after they submit an order.

## Choose between a company store and kits

A store is useful when employee selection is part of the intended experience. A kit is useful when your team wants to curate a defined set of merchandise. ShelfMerch supports stores and sending kits in the enterprise workflow described above.

You can discuss both approaches during the demo. Start with the purpose of the program and ask which workflow fits it. Keep the decision tied to the recipient experience, available products, budget, and operational responsibilities.

For example, an onboarding program may call for a curated welcome kit, while an ongoing merch initiative may emphasize employee choice. These are planning options, not a rule that every company should follow.

## Company swag store launch checklist

Before launch, confirm the following with your internal team and ShelfMerch:

- The program has a defined audience and purpose.
- The company admin and entity managers understand their responsibilities.
- Company and entity budgets have been discussed and allocated as required.
- The store contains the intended catalog products.
- Employee points and program instructions match the agreed setup.
- The employee redemption journey has been reviewed.
- Order processing and support expectations are understood.
- Any questions about product availability, point rules, or fulfillment have been resolved.

A company swag store needs a clear path from budget to employee choice to order processing. Planning those steps together gives your team a practical basis for the rollout.
